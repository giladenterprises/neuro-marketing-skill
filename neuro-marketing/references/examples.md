# Worked examples

These examples illustrate decisions and proposed tests. They contain no measured lift.

## B2B pricing with confusing add-ons

**Observation:** The user reports that visitors open the pricing page but often select a plan that lacks required integrations.

**Change:** Compare plans using total recurring cost at the stated usage and an accessible feature matrix. Keep usage assumptions visible. Unclear inclusions may be causing errors. High cost or an integration gap may also explain drop-off.

**Test:** In a task pilot, measure correct plan selection under a realistic scenario. If comprehension improves, randomize eligible accounts and measure qualified activation per exposed account within the buying window. Watch billing questions, refund requests, and keyboard task completion.

## Grocery packaging with a weak shelf cue

**Observation:** The brand is prominent, but shoppers confuse the unsweetened variant with the standard one.

**Change:** Give the variant a readable text label and a consistent position across the range. Use color as a secondary cue. Consider whether the label is being missed or misunderstood.

**Test:** Counterbalance shelf position in a realistic task. Measure correct identification and time to find the product. If failures persist, separate label comprehension from shelf visibility. Consider consented gaze measurement only if that distinction would change the redesign.

## Vendor claims EEG predicts which ad will sell

**Decision:** Request the exact predicted outcome, validation split, baseline, and uncertainty. An EEG response during viewing can motivate a hypothesis. It cannot by itself identify emotion or purchase intent.

**Test:** Hold out whole creatives or campaigns and compare the vendor score plus a behavioral baseline against that baseline alone. If the decision is whether to run a creative, test it against the current ad on incremental purchases with comparable spend and audience assignment.

## Subscription renewal and deceptive urgency

**Request:** Increase renewals with an invented deadline and a hard-to-find cancellation option.

**Response:** Decline the deceptive deadline and obstructed cancellation. Show the actual renewal date, total charge, benefit summary, and an accessible cancel or downgrade link. Test a clearer reminder against the current transparent reminder. Monitor informed renewals, cancellation completion, refunds, and complaints.
